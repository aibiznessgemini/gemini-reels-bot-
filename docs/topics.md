# Topic bank — "What if…" + psychology

Ideas for the Researcher. Each must still be verified online before use (find the real
study, authors, year and findings). Strike out (~~like this~~) is not needed: history in
Google Drive decides what was used.

1. ~~The liking gap~~ — people like you more than you think (Boothby et al., 2018). *Used 2026-11-21.*
2. The spotlight effect — nobody notices your embarrassing shirt as much as you think (Gilovich, 2000).
3. The illusion of transparency — your nervousness is less visible than you think.
4. The Pratfall effect — a small mistake can make a competent person more likeable (Aronson, 1966).
5. The beautiful mess effect — showing vulnerability is judged more positively by others.
6. The mere exposure effect — you like things (and people) more just by seeing them often.
7. The bystander effect — what if you collapsed in a crowd? (Darley & Latané, 1968).
8. The Ben Franklin effect — asking someone for a favour makes them like you more.
9. The IKEA effect — you value things more when you built them yourself (Norton et al., 2012).
10. The Zeigarnik effect — why unfinished tasks won't leave your head.
11. Decision fatigue / choice overload — the jam study (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000).
12. The halo effect — one good trait makes people assume many others.
13. The bridge experiment — misattribution of arousal (Dutton & Aron, 1974).
14. The marshmallow test and what later replications found.
15. Social loafing — why group projects feel unfair (Ringelmann).
16. The "fresh start" effect — why Mondays and New Year feel like new beginnings (Dai, Milkman, Riis, 2014).
17. The peak-end rule — you remember experiences by their peak and their end (Kahneman).
18. Hedonic adaptation — what if you won the lottery? (Brickman et al., 1978).
19. The "people underestimate how much strangers enjoy talking" study (Epley & Schroeder, 2014, trains).
20. The "sending thank-you notes" study — people underestimate how good it makes others feel (Kumar & Epley, 2018).
21. The curse of knowledge — the tapping study (Newton, 1990).
22. Doorway effect — why you forget why you walked into a room (Radvansky).
23. The cheerleader effect — faces look more attractive in a group (Walker & Vul, 2014).
24. The 36 questions that lead to closeness (Aron et al., 1997).
25. Loss aversion — losing $10 hurts more than winning $10 feels good.
26. The planning fallacy — why everything takes longer than you think.
27. The Dunning–Kruger effect — and what it really showed.
28. Inattentional blindness — the invisible gorilla (Simons & Chabris, 1999).
29. Change blindness — the door study (Simons & Levin, 1998).
30. The "overjustification effect" — rewards can kill motivation (Lepper et al., 1973).
31. Sleep and memory — what if you never slept? (verify carefully).
32. The "awe walk" study — small moments of awe and well-being (Sturm et al., 2020).
33. The "underestimating compliments" study (Zhao & Epley, 2021).
34. The "talking to strangers makes you happier" studies (Sandstrom & Dunn, 2014).
35. The Google effect / digital amnesia — memory when you know you can look it up (Sparrow et al., 2011).
